# Listen & Learn — The Secret of 'Ali's Force of Attraction

## Narration

The first part culminates in the secret of Ali's attraction. The central question is why a person can inspire devotion across time: the answer lies not in charm alone, but in the combination of truth, courage, justice, worship, and concern for people.

We will move through the concepts in sequence. Listen for the way each point contributes to the larger argument, and for the difference between what the source reports and what it asks the reader to infer.

### The question: what is the reason for the love for 'Ali in people's hearts? Nobody has discovered the secret — no one can formulate it as 'if

The question: what is the reason for the love for 'Ali in people's hearts? Nobody has discovered the secret — no one can formulate it as 'if it were like this, that would follow'. But it has one.

Notice the movement from an example to a general lesson. The historical detail matters because it tests an idea against real choices, where motives, pressure, and consequences cannot be separated. Attraction is not complete until it changes the direction of the self; the relationship should cultivate virtues rather than stop at admiration.

### Three explanations REJECTED: (a) it is not his body — his body is not among us and we never perceived it with our senses; (b) it is not hero

Three explanations REJECTED: (a) it is not his body — his body is not among us and we never perceived it with our senses; (b) it is not hero-worship, which exists in every nation; (c) it is a mistake to say it is love of moral and human excellence, i.e. a humanistic love.

A practical test follows from this point. A slogan may sound convincing on its own, but the fuller question is whether the interpretation is sound and whether the action that follows is just, proportionate, and faithful to the principle. The lesson is strongest when it becomes a standard for self-examination instead of a reason to judge others from a distance.

### Why (c) fails — the decisive counterfactual

Why (c) fails — the decisive counterfactual. It is true 'Ali was the manifestation of the perfect man and that men love great human figures. But had he possessed all those excellences — the wisdom and knowledge, self-sacrifice and altruism, humility and modesty, courtesy, kindness and mercy, protection of the weak, justness, liberality and love of freedom, respect for humanity, generosity, bravery, magnanimity and mercy toward enemies, munificence and beneficence — and had not had the divine touch in him, 'it is quite certain that there would not have been the feeling of sympathy and awakening of love that there is today'.

The source invites us to reason rather than merely react. Keep the claim, the evidence offered for it, and the author's conclusion distinct; that makes the argument easier to understand and to evaluate fairly. The lesson is strongest when it becomes a standard for self-examination instead of a reason to judge others from a distance.

### The answer: 'Ali is loved because he had the divine link

The answer: 'Ali is loved because he had the divine link. 'Our hearts are unconsciously completely involved with, and connected to, the Truth, right in their depths, and since they find 'Ali to be a great sign of the Truth and a manifestation of the attributes of the Truth they are in love with him.' The basis is the connection of our souls with the Truth laid in our primordial natures (fitrah) — and since our primordial natures are eternal, love for 'Ali is also eternal.

A practical implication follows: when we face a similar choice, we should ask what is actually at stake, whose interests are being served, and whether our response is guided by knowledge rather than impulse. Attraction is not complete until it changes the direction of the self; the relationship should cultivate virtues rather than stop at admiration.

### What gives him a 'resplendent and shining place for ever' is his faith and morality — that is what gives him his divine charisma.

What gives him a 'resplendent and shining place for ever' is his faith and morality — that is what gives him his divine charisma.

The argument here does more than preserve a detail. It gives us a criterion for judging conduct: we must look at the reason behind an action, the principle being claimed, and the effect that follows. The lesson is strongest when it becomes a standard for self-examination instead of a reason to judge others from a distance.

## Deep Reading Insight

A lasting moral legacy combines qualities that are often separated: tenderness without weakness, courage without cruelty, and firmness without vanity. Their unity helps explain the force of a principled character.
