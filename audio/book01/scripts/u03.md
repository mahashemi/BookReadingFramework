# Listen & Learn — Attraction and Repulsion in the World of Man

## Narration

Here the discussion moves from the physical world into human life. Human attraction and repulsion are not only reactions of the body; they can express affection, shared values, admiration, conscience, fear, prejudice, or a deliberate moral choice.

We will move through the concepts in sequence. Listen for the way each point contributes to the larger argument, and for the difference between what the source reports and what it asks the reader to infer.

### Scope limit: this is NOT about sexual attraction (a separate enquiry), and NOT about cooperation based on shared material benefit

Scope limit: this is NOT about sexual attraction (a separate enquiry), and NOT about cooperation based on shared material benefit. It is about friendship, affection, enmity and hatred in social life.

The source invites us to reason rather than merely react. ### The root cause is general resemblance (similarity) or contrariety (tadadd)

The root cause is general resemblance (similarity) or contrariety (tadadd). Metaphysics has proved that general resemblance is the cause of union.

A practical implication follows: when we face a similar choice, we should ask what is actually at stake, whose interests are being served, and whether our response is guided by knowledge rather than impulse. ### The rival theory — need: man is created essentially in want

The rival theory — need: man is created essentially in want. He is drawn to whoever fills his emptiness, repelled by whoever adds to it, and indifferent to whoever does neither. All inclination springs from the instinct for taking advantage.

Notice the movement from an example to a general lesson. The historical detail matters because it tests an idea against real choices, where motives, pressure, and consequences cannot be separated. ## Deep Reading Insight

Human maturity involves examining the reasons behind a reaction. Instinct may start a response, but conscience and understanding should decide whether it deserves to guide our conduct.
