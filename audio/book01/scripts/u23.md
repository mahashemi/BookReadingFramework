# Listen & Learn — The Politics of 'Using' the Qur'an & Fighting Hypocrisy

## Narration

The discussion turns to the misuse of religious language and scripture. A sacred phrase does not guarantee a sound conclusion; we must examine how it is interpreted, what conduct it justifies, and whether it is used to clarify truth or conceal a political aim.

### The technique has lasted thirteen centuries

The technique has lasted thirteen centuries. 'Holding the Qur'an upon a spear' becomes 'especially rife among those who wish to profit from it whenever sanctimoniousness and exotericism increases and it becomes fashionable to display one's piety and asceticism.'

### LESSON ONE — the ignorant as a ready-made instrument

LESSON ONE — the ignorant as a ready-made instrument. Whenever the ignorant and uninformed 'put on a show of sanctity and piety, and people take them to be the symbol of the practicing Muslim, an excellent tool is available for unscrupulous schemers'. Such schemers 'always turn these people into an instrument for their own ends, and make their presence a strong hindrance to the ideas of real reformers'. It is common to see anti-Islamic elements openly 'setting the power of Islam itself to work against Islam'.

### LESSON TWO — correct methodology of derivation

LESSON TWO — correct methodology of derivation. 'The Qur'an is a leader and a guide when it is subjected to true reflection, when it is interpreted wisely, when guidance is taken from the people who really know the Qur'an, who are firmly rooted in its sciences. As long as our methodology is wrong… we shall not derive any profit from it.' Profiteers or the ignorant read it and 'follow up an incorrect possibility' — Nahj al-Balaghah: 'they say the word “truth”, and then set their minds on falsehood!' ### Why the Qur'an gives universals, not names

Why the Qur'an gives universals, not names. It 'always presents its project in a general and fundamental form', and deducing the particular from the universal 'depends on our correct understanding'. It does not say that in a war on a certain day 'Ali was right; it says (49:9): If two parties of believers fight, put things right between them; then, if one of them is insolent against the other, fight the insolent one till it reverts to Allah's commandment. It 'does not spell out names', nor say that after forty years a man called Mu'awiyah will fight 'Ali and you should side with 'Ali. 'The Qur'an's task is not to make a list of subjects and point out which is right and which is wrong; such a thing would be impossible.' It came to stay forever, so it must make fundamentals clear 'so that falsehood can take its place face to face with truth in every age'.

The duty this imposes: people must open their eyes to the universal and distinguish the terrorizing party from the terrorized; accept it if the unruly party ceases; but if they 'stop, and try to be cunning so as to save themselves from defeat, and prepare themselves for a new attack', then be firm and do not give way to their cunning. 'It is up to the people themselves to discriminate in all these matters.' The Qur'an seeks that Muslims be intellectually and socially mature, and a necessary consequence of such maturity is the ability to differentiate between the just man and the unjust man. 'The Qur'an did not come to be always for people like a guardian over a juvenile.'

THE NECESSITY OF FIGHTING HYPOCRISY. 'The most difficult struggle is the one against hypocrisy, for it is the struggle against the cunning who use the stupid as their weapon.' It is 'several degrees more difficult than the fight with unbelief', because unbelief is 'an unconcealed, open and unhidden current', while the struggle against hypocrisy is a struggle against concealed unbelief.

### Hypocrisy has two faces: outward — Islam and Muslim; inward — unbelief and evil

Hypocrisy has two faces: outward — Islam and Muslim; inward — unbelief and evil. Spotting the latter is very difficult for ordinary people and sometimes impossible, so 'the struggle with hypocrisy ends in failure because the great majority of people cannot extend the reach of their perception beyond outward forms… They do not have a long enough range to penetrate the depths of the inward nature of things.'

## Deep Reading Insight

Religious language must not be allowed to end an argument before it begins. Responsible interpretation asks what the words mean in context and whether the resulting action serves the principle being invoked.
