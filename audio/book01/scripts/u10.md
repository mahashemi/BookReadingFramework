# Listen & Learn — Constructive or Destructive?

## Narration

The discussion now asks whether attraction and repulsion build or destroy. Neither force is automatically virtuous: attraction can attach someone to a harmful cause, and repulsion can become prejudice. Their value depends on the truth and justice that guide them.

We will move through the concepts in sequence. Listen for the way each point contributes to the larger argument, and for the difference between what the source reports and what it asks the reader to infer.

### Kind 1 — the passions

Kind 1 — the passions. Shared with animals, but in man reaches a peak of indescribable intensity, hence also called love. In reality 'a torrent, a bursting forth, a tempest of the passions'. It originates from sexuality and ends there; its rise and fall are largely tied to the physiological activity of the genital organs and to the years of youth; it diminishes with age, and with satiation and separation. A youth quivering at a beautiful face should know 'there is nothing more operating here than a material, animal process'. It comes quickly and goes quickly, cannot be depended on or recommended, has dangers, and kills virtue.

A practical test follows from this point. ### Kind 2 — noble sentiment, Qur'anically muwaddah wa rahmah (30:21)

Kind 2 — noble sentiment, Qur'anically muwaddah wa rahmah (30:21). Here the beloved receives respect and eminence; the lover seeks that person's happiness and is prepared to sacrifice for it. Produces purity, sincerity, tenderness, compassion, altruism — as opposed to the first kind which creates crudeness, savagery and criminality. Examples: a mother's love for her child; devotion to the pure ones and men of God; patriotism; love of principles.

But it must never be RECOMMENDED. It is 'a dangerous valley to enter'. ## Deep Reading Insight

The ethical question is always about the object, reason, and consequence of attraction or repulsion. Strong feeling without sound judgment can be as destructive as indifference.
