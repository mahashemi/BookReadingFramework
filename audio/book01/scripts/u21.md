# Listen & Learn — The Distinguishing Features of the Khawarij

## Narration

Here the author examines the Khawarij's mixed character: discipline and sacrifice existed alongside intellectual rigidity and severe misjudgment. The point is to understand how admirable traits can become dangerous when they are not guided by knowledge and sound moral reasoning.

FEATURE 1 — Struggle and self-sacrifice (positive). They strove valiantly for their beliefs; their history contains 'completely altruistic men who have few equals in the history of mankind'. ### FEATURE 2 — Worship and devotion (positive)

FEATURE 2 — Worship and devotion (positive). They spent nights in prayer, with no desire for the world. Ibn 'Abbas's report from Nahrawan: twelve thousand men whose foreheads were calloused by excess of prostration, whose hands had become hard like camels' feet from pressing the burning ground, whose shirts were tattered and worn down to their skins — 'but who were unwavering and determined'. They were strictly obedient to Islam's outward practices and never touched what they considered a sin. Ziyad ibn Abih killed one and asked his slave what kind of man he was: the slave said he had never brought him food by day nor laid his bedding at night — he fasted the days and prayed the nights.

### FEATURE 3 — Ignorance (negative)

FEATURE 3 — Ignorance (negative). They could not understand realities and wrongly interpreted events; this warped understanding hardened into a religion for which they made the greatest sacrifices. Historical explanation: in the whole thirteen-year Meccan period the Prophet permitted no jihad, not even defensive; permission came only in the second year in Medina. That interval let Islam's teachings and spirit 'penetrate into the depths of their spirits', so each companion became a true emissary who knew what he was fighting for. 'Ali: 'They linked their profound understanding with their swords.' But in the time of the caliphs, most attention went to conquests, neglecting the duty to teach Islamic culture and spirit to those newly attracted. The Khawarij — mostly Arabs, some not — were all ignorant of Islam's principles and unacquainted with its culture, and 'wanted to redress all their shortcomings by emphasis on prostration'.

### THE HUKM ERROR

THE HUKM ERROR. Qur'an 6:57: in al-hukmu illa li'llah — 'The judgement is Allah's alone.' Here hukm means the law and order of man's life, i.e. the right to lay down law, denied to any other than God (or one authorised by Him). The Khawarij took hukm as hukumah (government), also implying hakamiyah (arbitration) — concluding that government, arbitration and leadership, like law-giving, belong to God alone, and no one may arbitrate among or govern people.

'ALI'S REPLY: 'The sentence is right but what they think it means is wrong.' Law-giving is God's alone, but they say governance is. ### FEATURE 4 — Narrow-mindedness (negative)

FEATURE 4 — Narrow-mindedness (negative). 'Their thought evolved below very inferior horizons'; they 'enclosed Islam and the Muslims within the four walls of their own limited ideas' and claimed everyone else had misunderstood and was destined for Hell. The first thing such a person does is give his narrow-mindedness the form of a religious belief — restricting God's mercy, making Him 'sit forever on a throne of wrath, waiting for his slave to make some error'. Doctrine: the perpetrator of any great sin — lying, backbiting, drinking alcohol — is a kafir, outside Islam, eternally condemned.

'Ali's two-part refutation (Sermon 126): (1) The Prophet's practice — he punished someone and then read the burial prayers over his corpse, which would be forbidden (9:84) if great sin made one an infidel; he lashed the drinker, cut the thief's hand, whipped the adulterer, then gave them all a place in Muslim meetings, did not cut their wages from the bayt al-mal, and married them to other Muslims — meting out punishments without crossing names off the list of the Muslims. (2) The generalisation error — suppose 'Ali had erred and thereby become an infidel: why condemn the whole community? Why carry swords and subject the sinless and the sinners alike to their edge? So he objected on two counts: generalising sin to the guiltless, and equating sin with unbelief.

### FEATURE 5 — Inflexibility and the sacralising of forms

FEATURE 5 — Inflexibility and the sacralising of forms. They 'started an inflexibility and stagnation completely opposed to the Qur'an's way', conceiving Islamic education as 'deadening and motionless' and dragging 'solid forms and shapes into Islam'. Against this: Islam is never concerned with mere forms; its teachings are directed to spirit and meaning. 'No material means or outward form can occur in Islam with a “sacred” side' — which is precisely why Islam avoids collision with scientific and cultural development and conforms easily to the requirements of the times. Islam gives universals, which can take many outward manifestations without the truth changing; but harmonising truth with its outward referents 'is not such an easy matter that anyone can do it, for it needs penetrating perception and genuine understanding'.

### Qur'an vs men as arbitrators

Qur'an vs men as arbitrators. 'Ali: 'We did not name people as arbitrators, but we named the Qur'an as arbitrator. The Qur'an is a book, bound between two covers, and it does not speak. It therefore needs an interpreter. Only men can be such interpreters.' Refusing would have made them the party turning from the Book, since 4:59 commands referring disputes to Allah and the Prophet — Allah meaning decision by the Qur'an, the Prophet meaning his sunnah.
