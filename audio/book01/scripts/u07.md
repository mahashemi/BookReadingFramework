# Listen & Learn — Shi'ism, the Creed of Love

## Narration

This section connects Shi'ism with love, but the argument is deeper than sentiment or identity. Love becomes meaningful when it changes what a person admires, what they follow, and how faithfully they live by the principles represented by the person they love.

We will move through the concepts in sequence. Listen for the way each point contributes to the larger argument, and for the difference between what the source reports and what it asks the reader to infer.

### The greatest mark of distinction of Shi'ism over other sects is that its foundation and bedrock is love — present from the Prophet's own tim

The greatest mark of distinction of Shi'ism over other sects is that its foundation and bedrock is love — present from the Prophet's own time.

This is a useful place to pause. A slogan may sound convincing on its own, but the fuller question is whether the interpretation is sound and whether the action that follows is just, proportionate, and faithful to the principle. In particular, attraction is not complete until it changes the direction of the self; the relationship should cultivate virtues rather than stop at admiration.

### Aliyyun wa shi'atuhu humu 'l-fa'izun — ''Ali and his party (Shi'ah) will be the triumphant ones.' Footnote is important: as-Suyuti relates

'Aliyyun wa shi'atuhu humu 'l-fa'izun — ''Ali and his party (Shi'ah) will be the triumphant ones.' Footnote is important: as-Suyuti relates it in ad-Durr al-manthur under Surah al-Bayyinah v.7, from Ibn 'Asakir, from Jabir ibn 'Abdillah al-Ansari; also al-Manawi, al-Haythami, and Ibn Hajar in as-Sawa'iq al-muhriqah — i.e. Sunni sources.

The source invites us to reason rather than merely react. Keep the claim, the evidence offered for it, and the author's conclusion distinct; that makes the argument easier to understand and to evaluate fairly. In particular, the lesson is strongest when it becomes a standard for self-examination instead of a reason to judge others from a distance.

### Taking 'Ali as one's friend is the way of love

Taking 'Ali as one's friend is the way of love. Shi'ism's history is joined with 'a chain of entirely unknown people, devoted, full of love and self-sacrificing'.

A practical implication follows: when we face a similar choice, we should ask what is actually at stake, whose interests are being served, and whether our response is guided by knowledge rather than impulse. In particular, attraction is not complete until it changes the direction of the self; the relationship should cultivate virtues rather than stop at admiration.

The paradox: 'Ali applied the hudud — lashes, even amputation — and those punished did not turn away; their love did not diminish in the slightest.

Therefore 'Ali is the standard and criterion (mi'yar) for assaying human natures. A sound nature never takes offence at 'Ali even under his sword; a diseased nature never attaches to him even after great favours — 'for 'Ali is nothing but the embodiment of truth'.

### The amputation story: a good believing man fell into error and had the fingers of his right hand cut off

The amputation story: a good believing man fell into error and had the fingers of his right hand cut off. Holding the bleeding hand in his left, he walked away. Ibn al-Kawwa', a seditious Kharijite, approached 'with an air of utter compassion' to exploit it politically: 'Who cut your hand off?' The man answered with a sustained eulogy — chief of the Prophet's successors, leader of the untainted at the Resurrection, most righteous of believers, Imam of right guidance, first to reach the Gardens of Felicity, hero of the brave, giver of zakat, champion of Mecca. ## Deep Reading Insight

Love of a model becomes credible through imitation. If affection leaves judgment and conduct untouched, it has not yet reached the level of devotion described in this argument.
