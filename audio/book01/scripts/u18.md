# Listen & Learn — How the Khawarij Came Into Existence

## Narration

This section traces the emergence of the Khawarij as a historical movement. The narration follows the sequence from political crisis to slogans, from slogans to rigid interpretation, and from rigid interpretation to a movement that treated its own judgment as the measure of everyone else.

### Etymology: khawarij = 'rebels', from khuruj = revolt/insurrection

Etymology: khawarij = 'rebels', from khuruj = revolt/insurrection. The footnote distinguishes two near senses of kharaja 'ala: standing up to fight, and disobedience/insubordination. Because they based their rebellion on a belief and religious ideology they became a sect and the name stuck to them alone — later rebels without a creed were not called Khawarij. They never formed a government but did create a school of law and a literature.

### THE STRATAGEM AT SIFFIN

THE STRATAGEM AT SIFFIN. On the final day the battle was turning in 'Ali's favour; Mu'awiyah, with 'Amr ibn al-'As, 'was only one step away from defeat' and saw no escape 'except by having recourse to the creation of confusion'. He ordered Qur'ans raised on spear-points, to show they were people of prayer and the Book, and that the Book should arbitrate. Note: 'Ali had proposed this before and they had refused — it was now 'a subterfuge to rescue themselves from a sure defeat'.

### THE LEGAL ARGUMENT HE USED — human shields

THE LEGAL ARGUMENT HE USED — human shields. In the jihad section of Islamic law: if the enemy puts Muslim prisoners at the front as a shield and advances behind them, and there is no access to the attacking enemy except through killing those Muslims, then killing them becomes permissible for the vital interests of Islam. They too are soldiers of Islam and become martyrs; but blood money must be paid from the treasury to their relatives. 'Ali notes this is also a definite rule of international law. His a fortiori: if live Muslims may be struck through, then 'there can be no objection made to the paper and bindings of books' — respect for pages is because of their meaning, and today the fight is for that meaning.

### The refusal

The refusal. 'The ignorant and uninformed drew down a black curtain in front of their minds.' They answered that fighting the Qur'an is itself a sin and they must kill to prevent it. Only an hour was needed for victory: Malik al-Ashtar had gone out to destroy Mu'awiyah's command pavilion. This group threatened to attack 'Ali from behind, then drew their swords and threatened to hack him to pieces unless Malik was recalled. Malik replied that a few more moments would finish the enemy; word was sent that if he wanted to see 'Ali alive he must return. He returned — 'and the enemies were jubilant that their stratagem had proved efficacious'.

### THE ARBITRATION

THE ARBITRATION. 'Ali said they should choose their arbitrator and he would name his own. Without the slightest dispute they unanimously chose 'Amr ibn al-'As — the deviser of the stratagem. 'Ali proposed 'Abdullah ibn al-'Abbas (versed in politics) or Malik al-Ashtar. But 'those fools were looking for someone of their own kind' and insisted on Abu Musa al-Ash'ari — 'a man of no perspicacity who was not on good terms with 'Ali'. 'Ali and his friends tried to enlighten them; they refused anyone else; he told them to do as they wished.

### THE DOUBLE-CROSS

THE DOUBLE-CROSS. After months, 'Amr proposed that neither 'Ali nor Mu'awiyah be caliph but a third — surely 'Abdullah ibn 'Umar, Abu Musa's son-in-law. Agreed: 'You should remove 'Ali, and I will do the same with Mu'awiyah.' At the assembly 'Amr deferred with flattery — 'You are the respected, white-bearded man, a companion of the Prophet. Never would I presume to speak before you!' Abu Musa deposed 'Ali, removing his ring from his finger as the image. 'Amr then rose, confirmed 'Ali's deposition, and moved his ring from his right hand to his left, saying: 'I set up Mu'awiyah in the caliphate, just as I put this ring on my finger.' Uproar: the people attacked Abu Musa and beat him with whips; he fled to Mecca and 'Amr went to Damascus.

### THE MISDIAGNOSIS — the birth of the sect

THE MISDIAGNOSIS — the birth of the sect. The Khawarij saw the scandal and realised their mistake, 'but they could not understand where exactly their error lay'. They did not say their error was falling for the stratagem and halting the war; nor that it was choosing Abu Musa. Instead: appointing any human arbitrator in God's religion was itself unbelief, for the judge is only God, not man.

### The demand on 'Ali: 'We did not understand… You have become an unbeliever, and so have we

The demand on 'Ali: 'We did not understand… You have become an unbeliever, and so have we. But we repent; you too should repent.' 'Ali: 'In any situation, repentance is good. We are always repenting for our sins.' Not enough — he must confess arbitration was a sin and repent of that. He refused: it was they who brought it about, and he could not declare sinful what Islam made lawful, nor confess a sin he had not committed.

### BIDDING TO GOOD — the two conditions

BIDDING TO GOOD — the two conditions. Al-amr bi'l-ma'ruf requires (1) profound, knowledgeable insight into the religion, and (2) profound insight into how to act. The second depends on two jurisprudential conditions: ihtimal al-ta'thir (the possibility of effective action) and 'adam tarattub al-mafsadah 'alayh (absence of any resulting evil). Without profound religious knowledge, hadith teaches the loss exceeds the benefit.

Why this duty is uniquely conditional: for prayer, fasting, hajj, zakat and jihad, one does not ask whether a useful result will follow — benefit exists in every duty but discerning it is not the people's responsibility. In bidding to good it is their responsibility: one must look to the result, the reaction, and whether the action serves the interests of Islam and the Muslims. All Islamic sects agree on this condition except the Khawarij, who held it absolute and unconditional — 'one must not sit down and think about it'. Hence they 'terrorized the lands knowing that they would be killed and their blood would be wasted, and knowing that no useful result would come out of their uprising'.

## Deep Reading Insight

Sincerity cannot replace knowledge. A person may be intensely committed and still misread a situation, misuse a principle, and cause harm while believing that the harm is righteous.
