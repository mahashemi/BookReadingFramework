# Book 02 — Speaker Script & Exam Questions

## Core speaking pattern
**Problem → Claim → Example/Evidence → Distinction → Implication → Retrieval**

This is the teacher-facing “what to actually say” version of the Book 02 material.

---

# Session 1 — The Puzzle

“Why do the Imams appear to use such different methods if they all guide to one religion?

al-Hassan makes peace. al-Husayn refuses submission. Zayn al-'Abidin is known for worship and service. al-Sadiq does not seize a political opening. Musa al-Kazim resists from prison. al-Rida becomes crown prince. al-'Askari leads under surveillance.

Mutahhari's starting point is that outward method must be read in context. The enduring purpose of religion does not require identical behavior in every circumstance.”

**Teach:** al-jam' al-'urfi; stable principles vs changing circumstances; clothing and beard-dyeing examples.

**Ask:** “What is the danger of comparing the Imams by outward action alone?”

---

# Session 2 — 'Ali and the Kharijites

“'Ali gives us a test case. Justice is not something he suspends simply because politics becomes difficult.

Then Siffin gives us the opposite problem: a religious symbol is placed in the service of a political move. The Qur'ans on spears stop people from thinking about what the Qur'an actually requires.

The Kharijites then turn rigid interpretation into a sectarian weapon. Their outward worship is impressive, but Mutahhari emphasizes their lack of understanding.

The lesson is simple: **religious appearance is not identical to religious understanding.**”

**Example:** Qur'ans on spears.

**Example:** Iqbal/Ja'far-Sadiq rumor.

**Ask:** “Why does Mutahhari insist that people investigate before judging?”

---

# Session 3 — al-Hassan and al-Husayn

“Do not begin by calling one peaceful and the other rebellious. Begin with jurisprudence and historical circumstances.

The Prophet himself moved between restraint, warfare and treaty. Hudaybiyyah is especially important because a treaty that looked costly to some companions created conditions favorable to the larger mission.

Now compare the two Imams: office, military balance, Kufah, allegiance, and the condition of the ruling regime.”

Put on board:

**al-Hassan:** sitting caliph → rebellion → exhausted Kufah → unfavorable military conditions → no personal allegiance demand.

**al-Husayn:** no governing office → entrenched rule → allegiance demanded → mass invitation from Kufah → more developed evidence of oppression.

**Ask:** “Why can peace be a reasoned response rather than weakness?”

---

# Session 4 — Spiritual Resistance

“Not every form of resistance is a battlefield.

Zayn al-'Abidin combines worship, service, compassion and preservation of Karbala's memory.

Musa al-Kazim combines dignity, endurance, refusal to submit and spiritual influence. His influence is visible in people who encounter him even inside the prison system.”

**Examples:** 'Isa ibn Ja'far, Bishr Hafi, Safwan Jammal.

**Ask:** “What changes when we define resistance more broadly than armed revolt?”

---

# Session 5 — Political Openings

“An opportunity to take power is not automatically a duty to take power.

The Abbasid movement uses broad Islamic and Ahl al-Bayt slogans. Abu Salmah's double letter makes the political nature of the opening especially visible. Imam al-Sadiq asks the deeper question: are the real conditions present for a sound movement?”

Then move to al-Rida:

“Now the Imam is offered a position inside the ruling system. The key is to separate the historical facts from explanations about motive.”

Teach:

**certain facts:** Ma'mun initiated the proposal; al-Rida resisted; acceptance came under pressure; non-interference was required.

**disputed:** exact motive; role of Fadl ibn Sahl; whether the appointment was meant to discredit the Imam.

**Ask:** “Why is it dangerous to turn a proposed historical motive into a proven fact?”

---

# Session 6 — al-'Askari

“Here the political problem becomes surveillance itself.

Samarra, military surroundings, intense monitoring, searches for the expected child, concealment, and the role of family members all show how the outward form of Imamate changes under pressure.”

Board:

**surveillance → concealment → protection → continuity**

**Ask:** “What is the paradox at the heart of this chapter?”

---

# Session 7 — Universal Justice

“Chapter 8 changes scale from individual Imams to the future of humanity.

Justice is not merely a promise about the afterlife. Mutahhari discusses justice as part of the prophetic mission and asks whether human beings can rise above immediate self-interest.

'Ali then becomes a concrete example of the abstract principle: justice is lived in decisions about power, rights and people.”

Bridge:

**justice → human perfection → future universal order**

**Ask:** “What is the difference between justice and simply treating everyone identically?”

---

# Session 8 — Mahdism and Active Awaiting

“Mahdism is presented not as an idea that suddenly appeared with the birth of the final Imam. Mutahhari discusses Qur'anic foundations, prophetic reports, Sunni and Shi'ah narrations and the historical record of Mahdi claimants.

But the history of false claimants leads to an important distinction:

**belief in Mahdism ≠ acceptance of every claimant.**

Then comes the practical question: should corruption be allowed to grow so that reappearance comes sooner?

Mutahhari rejects that idea. Awaiting does not cancel prayer, fasting, education, reform, social responsibility or enjoining good.”

Final line:

**“Awaiting is not an excuse to abandon responsibility.”**

---

# Final retrieval

Ask without notes:

1. What is the central problem of the Introduction?
2. What does al-jam' al-'urfi mean?
3. What made the Kharijite problem dangerous?
4. Why is Hudaybiyyah relevant to al-Hassan?
5. What were the major differences between al-Hassan and al-Husayn?
6. Give one example of spiritual resistance.
7. Why did al-Sadiq reject the political opening?
8. Which part of the al-Rida story is certain, and which part remains disputed?
9. Why was al-'Askari's environment unusual?
10. How does justice connect to Mahdism?
11. Why does awaiting not cancel duty?
12. Give the whole-book argument in one paragraph.

## Teacher loop

When the student gives a fact, ask:

**“Why does Mutahhari tell us this?”**

When the student gives an interpretation, ask:

**“What evidence supports it?”**

When the student gives a slogan, ask:

**“What distinction does the chapter make?”**
