# Book 02 — Teaching Guide
## A Survey into the Lives of the Infallible Imams

This is the teacher-facing companion to the Book 02 study system.

The teaching principle is the same one used successfully in the Book 01 lecture materials:

**Claim → explanation → example/evidence → implication → exam question**

Do not teach the chapters as isolated biographies. Teach the recurring problem Mutahhari is solving: **the Imams may act differently because their circumstances differ, while the underlying religious purpose remains continuous.**

---

## Teaching architecture

### Block 1 — The central problem
Introduction + the al-Hassan/al-Husayn question.

**Big question:** Why can two Imams appear to choose opposite methods without their principles being opposite?

**Core answer:** Method is conditioned by circumstances; principles are not simply reducible to one outward behavior.

### Block 2 — 'Ali and the problem of superficial religiosity
Chapter 1.

**Big question:** Why can sincere-looking religiosity become dangerous when understanding is shallow?

Use the Kharijite story to teach the difference between:
- outward worship
- genuine understanding
- obedience to the Imam
- rigid slogan-based judgment

### Block 3 — al-Hassan and al-Husayn
Chapter 2 Sessions 1–2.

**Big question:** When is fighting obligatory, when is peace permissible, and why did the two Imams face different duties?

Teach:
- categories of jihad
- rebellion against a just ruler
- truce/armistice conditions
- Hudaybiyyah
- political position
- military balance
- allegiance demand
- Kufah's invitation
- treaty terms

### Block 4 — Spiritual resistance
Chapter 3 + Chapter 5.

**Big question:** Can worship, service, moral influence and refusal under pressure be forms of historical resistance?

Use:
- Zayn al-'Abidin's worship
- service during Hajj
- mourning as preservation of Karbala's message
- Musa al-Kazim's imprisonment
- 'Isa ibn Ja'far
- Bishr Hafi
- Safwan Jammal
- refusal to legitimize Harun

### Block 5 — Political openings and political traps
Chapter 4 + Chapter 6.

**Big question:** Does an opportunity to seize power automatically make political action correct?

Teach:
- Abbasid slogans
- Abu Muslim
- Abu Salmah
- Muhammad Nafs al-Zakiyyah
- Imam al-Sadiq's refusal
- al-Rida's crown-prince appointment
- certain facts vs disputed motives
- cooperation vs support
- conditional participation

### Block 6 — Imamate under surveillance
Chapter 7.

**Big question:** What does an oppressive political environment do to the outward form of religious leadership?

Teach:
- Samarra
- military surveillance
- six-year Imamate
- searches for the promised child
- concealed birth
- Jaddah
- Hakimah Khatun

### Block 7 — Universal justice
Chapter 8 Part 1.

**Big question:** What does justice mean beyond merely saying that the good will be rewarded in the afterlife?

Teach:
- justice as a general prophetic mission
- justice vs discrimination
- human nature
- self-interest theories
- Islamic account of moral capacity
- 'Ali as an example
- Mahdism and human perfection

### Block 8 — Mahdism and active awaiting
Chapter 8 Part 2.

**Big question:** What is the historical evidence for Mahdism, and what should belief in reappearance make a person do now?

Teach:
- pre-third-century Mahdism
- Qur'anic foundations
- Sunni and Shi'ah narrations
- historical Mahdi claims
- false claimants
- political appropriation
- evidence vs interpretation
- reform vs chaos
- awaiting does not cancel religious duties

# Session template

For a 45–60 minute class:

### 1. Retrieval — 5 minutes
Ask 3–5 questions from the previous lesson before teaching anything new.

### 2. Problem — 5 minutes
Put one apparent contradiction or difficult question on the board.

### 3. Claim — 5 minutes
State Mutahhari's governing claim clearly.

### 4. Evidence — 15–20 minutes
Tell 2–3 stories/examples rather than listing facts.

### 5. Distinction — 5–10 minutes
Make the key comparison visually.

### 6. Retrieval — 5 minutes
Students answer without notes.

### 7. Exam bridge — 5 minutes
Turn the lesson into SHORT and LONG questions.

# High-value teaching questions

## Introduction
1. Why is it dangerous to compare the Imams only by outward action?
2. What does taqiyyah mean in the context of the introduction?
3. How does Mutahhari reconcile differences in lifestyle?
4. What does al-jam' al-'urfi accomplish?

## 'Ali
5. Why did 'Ali initially resist accepting the caliphate?
6. What made the Kharijites different from ordinary political opponents?
7. What happened at Siffin when Qur'ans were raised on spears?
8. Why did the Kharijites later call arbitration blasphemy?
9. Why did 'Ali tolerate them before they became an armed threat?
10. What lesson does Mutahhari draw from the Iqbal/Ja'far-Sadiq anecdote?

## al-Hassan / al-Husayn
11. What are the main categories of jihad described in Session 1?
12. What conditions make a truce permissible?
13. Why is Hudaybiyyah important to Mutahhari's argument?
14. What three major differences separate al-Hassan's situation from al-Husayn's?
15. What were the key terms of al-Hassan's peace treaty?

## Zayn al-'Abidin
16. How does worship become a form of service?
17. Why did Zayn al-'Abidin prefer to serve people who did not know his identity?
18. How does Mutahhari interpret his mourning for al-Husayn?

## al-Sadiq
19. Why did Imam al-Sadiq reject Abu Salmah's political opening?
20. What is the difference between a genuine call to resist oppression and a Mahdi slogan?
21. Why does Mutahhari emphasize real political conditions?

## Musa al-Kazim
22. Why did Harun regard Musa al-Kazim as dangerous?
23. How did Musa al-Kazim influence people without leading an open revolt?
24. What does the Bishr Hafi story illustrate?
25. What does the Safwan Jammal story illustrate?

## al-Rida
26. Which facts about the crown-prince episode are presented as certain?
27. Which motives of Ma'mun remain disputed?
28. Why did al-Rida impose a non-interference condition?
29. What does the 'Id prayer episode demonstrate?
30. How does the text distinguish cooperation from support of an oppressive ruler?

## al-'Askari
31. Why was surveillance particularly intense?
32. Why was the birth of the expected child concealed?
33. Why is Jaddah important in the narrative?

## Justice
34. How does Mutahhari define universal justice?
35. Why does he reject the idea that justice is only an afterlife promise?
36. How does he distinguish justice from discrimination?
37. What competing accounts of human nature does he discuss?

## Mahdism
38. Why does Mutahhari begin with historical development rather than only doctrine?
39. What is the significance of Sunni narrations about Mahdism?
40. Why is the existence of false Mahdi claimants not evidence against Mahdism itself?
41. Why does Mutahhari reject the idea that corruption should be allowed to grow so that reappearance comes sooner?
42. Why does awaiting reappearance leave personal and social duties intact?

# Teacher warning labels

### Do not flatten uncertainty
Especially in Chapter 6, distinguish:
**historical fact → reported interpretation → Mutahhari's argument**

### Do not turn every story into an independent doctrine
Some stories are illustrations of a larger argument.

### Do not teach “peace = weakness” or “war = courage”
The whole Chapter 2 argument exists partly to reject that simplistic binary.

### Do not teach “Mahdism = passivity”
The final section explicitly connects awaiting with continuing religious responsibility and reform.

---

# End-of-book synthesis

Ask students to explain this chain from memory:

**different circumstances**
→ **different outward methods**
→ **same religious purpose**
→ **different forms of resistance**
→ **spiritual + social + political dimensions**
→ **justice**
→ **Mahdism**
→ **active responsibility while awaiting**

That chain is the conceptual backbone of the whole teaching package.


# Live listener-question method

Do not wait for students to ask questions spontaneously. Build the questions into the teaching itself.

For each major idea, use this sequence:

**State the claim → tell the example → pause → provoke an objection → make students explain the distinction → return to the claim.**

Good prompts are questions such as:
- “What exactly is the author trying to prove with this story?”
- “What would change if the circumstances were different?”
- “Does this example actually prove the claim, or only illustrate it?”
- “What is the difference between these two cases?”
- “What would be the wrong conclusion if we read this too literally?”
- “Can someone challenge this argument before I answer it?”

A student should not receive full credit merely for naming the person or event. Ask for **meaning + evidence + reason + implication**.

## Session-specific provocation prompts

### Session 1 — The puzzle
**Question:** If the religion's principles are stable, why can two Imams act differently?

**Challenge:** Does “different circumstances” explain everything, or do we need a rule for deciding which circumstances matter?

### Session 2 — 'Ali and the Kharijites
**Question:** Can a person look extremely religious and still fundamentally misunderstand religion?

**Challenge:** Was the Kharijite problem only ignorance, or did certainty without understanding make the ignorance more dangerous?

### Session 3 — al-Hassan and al-Husayn
**Question:** If al-Husayn's refusal is courageous, what does that tell us about al-Hassan's peace?

**Challenge:** Which single change in circumstances would most alter the judgment of the decision: military strength, allegiance demand, Kufah's support, or the ruler's conduct?

### Session 4 — spiritual resistance
**Question:** How can worship or serving strangers become a form of historical resistance?

**Challenge:** What would be lost if we defined resistance only as fighting?

### Session 5 — political openings
**Question:** When does entering government become cooperation, and when does it become endorsement?

**Challenge:** Why must we separate a documented event from an explanation of the ruler's motive?

### Session 6 — al-'Askari
**Question:** What changes when political surveillance becomes the dominant circumstance?

**Challenge:** Does concealment mean weakness, or can concealment itself be a deliberate strategy of continuity?

### Session 7 — justice
**Question:** Is justice the same thing as treating everybody identically?

**Challenge:** If human beings were driven only by self-interest, what would that do to the possibility of a just society?

### Session 8 — Mahdism
**Question:** If the future just order is promised, why should people reform the present?

**Challenge:** Could deliberately allowing corruption ever be a valid form of “preparing” for reappearance? What argument does Mutahhari give against that?
